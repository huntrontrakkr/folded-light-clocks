Folded Light
Watch the film

One small LED shines through a stack of clear acrylic plates. Each plate carries a gentle relief, less than two millimetres deep. As the plates turn, the light they bend gathers into a picture on the wall: on the hour (or the double-hour) it holds still for a minute and a half, then the plates move on one after another, the picture melts, and the light wanders until the next one gathers.

What you see is the light of the designed plates, traced live in your browser: rays from a 1 mm white LED, through the plates at the angles the clock's motors hold at this moment. It is a simulation, faithful to the exact optical model of the designs to a correlation of 0.99; the clocks themselves are still being built.

The minute is the slim mark outside the picture; the seconds walk round the ring of dots. Tap the clock face, or "full screen", to see nothing else; tap again to return.

Sound. "Ambient" is a generative score from the films' instruments: slow phrases on cycles that never line up the same way twice, and a bell as each picture gathers. "The light" turns the light itself into sound: twenty-four voices each carry an equal share of it, pitched by how high on the wall that share falls, as if the light were a spectrogram. As the light moves the voices glide by the shortest path, the same optimal transport that shaped the plates, and the loudness never changes, because no light is lost. When the picture stands still, they settle into its chord.
